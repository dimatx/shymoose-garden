Siberian cypress, also called Russian arborvitae, is a dwarf, evergreen
conifer endemic to the Sikhote-Alin mountains of far-eastern Russia, where it
grows as a ground-hugging shrub above the treeline. In the garden it forms a
dense, spreading carpet of soft, feathery foliage with indefinitely
lengthening stems that nod gently at the tips — one of the toughest and most
reliable conifer ground covers for cold climates.

## Through the seasons

The scale-like leaves are arranged in flat, fan-like sprays reminiscent of
arborvitae, giving the whole plant a soft, textured look. Foliage is bright
green through spring and summer, then shifts to an attractive bronze-purple
as temperatures drop, adding real winter interest to a bed that would
otherwise look bare. Tiny, spherical, berry-like cones appear but are easy to
miss — this plant's appeal is entirely about its foliage and form.

## Care tips

Siberian cypress asks for very little: plant it in well-drained soil (it
will not tolerate "wet feet") in full sun to part shade, and otherwise leave
it alone. It performs best in cooler summer climates and can struggle where
summers are hot and humid. Because it tolerates more shade than most
ground-cover junipers, it's a useful alternative wherever a low, spreading
evergreen is needed on a bank, slope, rock garden, or foundation planting
that doesn't get all-day sun.

> **Heads up:** Good drainage is the one non-negotiable. Root rot in
> waterlogged soil is by far the most common way to lose this otherwise
> nearly indestructible plant.
