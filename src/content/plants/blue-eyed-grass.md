*Sisyrinchium angustifolium* 'Lucerne' is a compact, clump-forming perennial native to damp woods, meadows, and stream banks across eastern North America. Despite its narrow, grass-like foliage, it's actually a member of the iris family (Iridaceae) — and each stem is topped in late spring with small, six-pointed, violet-blue flowers centered on a golden-yellow eye.

## Bigger blooms, better behavior

'Lucerne' is prized for flowers noticeably larger than the wild species — up to an inch across — and for self-seeding less aggressively, making it a tidier choice for borders, rock gardens, and small-space plantings. Its clumping habit and neat foliage work well as an edging plant or massed in drifts, where the star-shaped flowers open in the morning sun.

## Care tips

Plant in full sun to part shade in well-drained soil with average to consistent moisture; good drainage is important to prevent crown rot over winter. Shear the clump back after flowering to tidy its appearance and curb self-seeding, and divide every two to three years to keep plants vigorous. It's low-maintenance and largely untroubled by pests or disease.
