*Campanula persicifolia* — peachleaf bellflower — is a clump-forming herbaceous perennial native to meadows and shrubby slopes across Europe and western Asia. The species name *persicifolia* means "having leaves like a peach," a reference to the narrow, leathery, bright-green foliage arranged in basal rosettes. 'Takion Blue' is a compact, mounded selection reaching just 1–2 feet, with lavender-blue, upward- and outward-facing bells appearing in June and July.

## A classic cottage garden flower

Tall, slender stems rise from the basal leaves and carry loose racemes of large, bell-shaped flowers that hold their own as cut flowers in a vase. Unlike some bellflowers, 'Takion Blue' is notably tidy — its mounded habit and compact height make it well-suited to the front of a perennial border or a cottage-style cutting garden. Bees, butterflies, and hummingbirds are regular visitors.

## Care tips

Peachleaf bellflower is unfussy in average, well-drained soil and tolerates a wide hardiness range (zones 3–7). In hot summer climates (zone 7), give it afternoon shade and extra water. Deadhead spent blooms promptly to extend the flowering season and prevent excessive self-seeding. Clumps can be divided every 2–4 years in spring to keep them vigorous.
