Italian parsley, also called flat-leaf parsley, is prized by cooks worldwide for its bold, clean flavor that holds up to heat far better than the curly-leaved variety. It forms a neat rosette of deeply divided, bright green leaves in its first year, making it equally at home at the edge of an ornamental border and in a dedicated herb bed. Plants prefer cool weather and grow best in spring and fall; in hot climates they benefit from afternoon shade and consistent watering to delay bolting.

Sow seeds directly outdoors 3–4 weeks before the last frost, or start indoors 6–8 weeks earlier. Note that germination is notoriously slow — up to 3–4 weeks — and soaking seeds overnight can help. Because parsley is a biennial, plants allowed to overwinter will send up tall flower stalks in their second year, at which point the leaves become coarser and lose their best flavor. Most gardeners treat it as an annual and replant each spring. Harvest by snipping the oldest outer stems at the base to encourage continuous new growth from the center. Italian parsley is also an important host plant for black swallowtail butterfly caterpillars, and its second-year flowers attract a range of beneficial insects to the garden.
