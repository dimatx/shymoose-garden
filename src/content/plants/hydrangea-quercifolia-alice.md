*Hydrangea quercifolia* 'Alice' is a University of Georgia selection of our native oakleaf hydrangea, grown for its oversized, cone-shaped flower panicles and bold, oak-shaped foliage. It's a big shrub that earns its space as a specimen or informal hedge, with three seasons of interest: early-summer bloom, deep fall color, and exfoliating winter bark.

## A native with presence

The deeply lobed leaves resemble oak leaves (hence the name) and can reach 8–12 inches long. In early to midsummer, dense pyramidal flower clusters open creamy white and gradually age through pink to tan, often persisting on the plant well into fall. Come autumn, the foliage turns a rich bronze-maroon to purple before dropping to reveal attractive cinnamon-colored, peeling bark for winter interest.

## Care tips

Give it morning sun and afternoon shade for the best flowering without leaf scorch, and consistently moist, well-drained, organically rich soil. Because it blooms on old wood, prune only immediately after flowering — cutting in late winter or spring sacrifices that year's blooms. Established plants are quite drought tolerant and largely trouble-free, with occasional aphids or powdery mildew being only minor issues.
