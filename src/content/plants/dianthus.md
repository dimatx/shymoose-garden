*Dianthus* is a large genus in the carnation family (Caryophyllaceae) with more than 27,000 registered cultivar names, grown as annuals, biennials and — most usefully in the garden — evergreen perennials. The group includes cottage pinks, cheddar pinks, sweet William and the florist's carnation. Most form neat cushions or mats of narrow, often glaucous blue-green leaves that look good even out of flower.

## Fringed, clove-scented flowers

The flowers are the draw: five petals with the characteristic fringed or "pinked" margin, frequently with a contrasting eye, carried above the foliage from late spring into summer. Many are powerfully clove-scented. They are excellent small cut flowers and a magnet for bees and butterflies.

## Care tips

Pinks are sun-lovers that demand sharp drainage and good airflow — the quickest way to lose one is heavy, wet soil over winter. Give them lean, neutral-to-alkaline ground, shear lightly after flowering to tidy the mound and coax a rebloom, and divide congested clumps every few years. They are happily ignored by deer. Note that while the petals are edible when grown without pesticides, the foliage is mildly toxic and can irritate sensitive skin.
