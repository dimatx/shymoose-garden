Purple coneflower is a sturdy, long-blooming herbaceous perennial in the daisy
family (Asteraceae), native to prairies, meadows, and open woods across
central and eastern North America. It's one of the most reliable native
perennials for a sunny border, forming upright clumps of coarse, rough-textured
leaves topped by daisy-like flowers from early summer well into fall.

## Through the seasons

The pinkish-purple ray petals droop slightly around a raised, spiny, brownish
cone of tiny disc flowers — the source of the genus name *Echinacea*, Greek
for "hedgehog" or "sea urchin." Each flower lasts for weeks, and established
clumps rebloom steadily if deadheaded. Left alone, the cones ripen into seed
heads that goldfinches and other birds visit through fall and winter.

## Care tips

Purple coneflower tolerates heat, humidity, drought, and a wide range of
soils once established, asking for little beyond full sun and decent
drainage. It self-seeds readily, so expect volunteer seedlings nearby — thin
them out or let a natural drift form. In late winter, cut the previous
year's dead stems back to about 12–24 inches rather than to the ground;
native bees overwinter and nest inside the hollow stems, so leaving that
stubble standing (even loosely piled nearby) supports them until they
naturally break down.

> **Heads up:** Purple coneflower is a magnet for pollinators, especially
> butterflies, and its seed heads are a favorite of finches — leave a few
> flower heads unclipped each season if you want the birds to visit.
