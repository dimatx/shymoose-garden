*Monarda didyma* 'Balbalmose' — sold as **Balmy™ Rose** — is a compact, modern selection of scarlet bee balm, a clump-forming perennial in the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to eastern North America. Where the wild species can flop at 3–4 feet, this one stays a neat 10–12 inches, making it easy to place at the front of a border or in a container while keeping all the pollinator appeal.

## A magnet for pollinators

Through the summer it carries rounded, slightly shaggy heads of rose-pink, two-lipped tubular flowers, each subtended by a whorl of leafy bracts. Hummingbirds, long-tongued bees and butterflies work the blooms constantly, and the aromatic foliage is left alone by deer and rabbits.

## Care tips

Bee balm's one weakness is powdery mildew, which strikes hardest on dry, crowded, poorly ventilated plants. This Balmy-series selection was bred for strong mildew resistance, but you'll still get the best results in rich, evenly moist soil with good air movement and full sun. Deadhead to extend bloom, divide every couple of years to keep clumps fresh, and consider leaving some hollow stems standing over winter — native bees nest inside them.
