*Muenchhusia moscheutos* (formerly classified as *Hibiscus moscheutos*), known
as rose mallow, is a native wetland perennial that produces some of the
biggest flowers you'll find in a perennial border — broad, saucer-shaped
blooms in shades of pink, red, or white, each with a distinctive dark eye at
the center. Despite its tropical appearance, it's fully hardy and dies back to
the ground each winter, re-emerging late in spring to put on its dramatic
summer show.

## A native for wet spots

In the wild, rose mallow grows in marshes, wet meadows, and along ponds and
streams throughout the eastern and southeastern U.S. It's an excellent choice
for rain gardens or any spot that stays consistently moist, though it adapts
well to average garden soil as long as it gets regular water.

## Care tips

Plant in full sun in rich, moisture-retentive soil for the heaviest bloom.
Because it emerges quite late in spring, mark its spot to avoid accidentally
disturbing the crown while other perennials are already up. Cut the dead
stems to the ground in late fall, or leave them standing through winter for
structure, and expect the whole plant to reappear reliably once the soil
warms.
