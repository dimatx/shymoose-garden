*Amsonia* 'String Theory' is a compact bluestar introduced by Proven Winners
for gardeners who love the feathery look of Arkansas bluestar but need a
shorter, tidier plant. The breeder markets it as an amsonia hybrid, while
reference sites often describe it as very closely allied to *Amsonia
hubrichtii*; either way, its threadlike foliage, cool blue flowers, and golden
fall color all read unmistakably as threadleaf bluestar.

In late spring, rounded clusters of pale periwinkle-blue flowers float above a
fine-textured mound that stays attractive long after bloom. Through summer the
foliage brings a soft, almost grassy texture to the border, and by autumn it
shifts to bright gold. Butterflies and other pollinators visit the flowers,
while deer usually leave the plant alone.

Give it a sunny to lightly shaded spot with decent drainage and avoid pushing
it with overly rich soil, which can make the stems open up. Once established it
handles normal dry spells well, though it looks fullest with average moisture.
If the clump loosens after bloom, trim it lightly to neaten the mound; otherwise
leave it alone until late winter. Like other amsonias, broken stems bleed a
milky sap, so gloves are a good idea when cutting it back.
