Oakleaf hydrangea is an upright, multi-stemmed, deciduous shrub native to
moist woodlands and stream banks of the southeastern United States. This
cultivar keeps the species' hallmark oak-shaped foliage and huge mophead
flower clusters, prized as a specimen or accent shrub wherever there's room
for its full size.

## Through the seasons

In early to mid-summer, the shrub is topped with large, dense, cone-shaped
panicles of white flowers that age gracefully to green and then a soft
pinkish-tan, remaining showy for weeks even as they fade. The large,
deeply-lobed leaves resemble oak foliage — hence the name — and turn a
striking burgundy to wine-red in fall. On mature stems, reddish-brown bark
exfoliates in winter, adding an extra season of textural interest once the
leaves have dropped.

## Care tips

Give this shrub organically rich, well-drained soil with consistent
moisture, and some daily sun — it tolerates more shade than most hydrangeas,
but flowering and fall color are both better with a few hours of direct
light. Because it blooms on old wood, prune only right after flowering; a
late-winter or spring pruning removes the coming season's flower buds. In
hot climates, afternoon shade and mulch help it cope with summer stress.

> **Heads up:** This hydrangea needs room — at 6 to 8 feet tall and wide,
> plan its spot as a specimen or informal hedge rather than a foundation
> plant, unless you have the space to let it reach full size.
