*Acorus calamus* 'Variegatus' — variegated sweet flag — is a clumping,
rhizomatous perennial grown for its narrow, arching, sword-shaped leaves
striped boldly in cream and green, with reddish leaf bases that add color
even before the foliage unfurls. It reads like an ornamental grass or a
small iris, but is happiest with far wetter feet than either.

## A plant for wet ground

Sweet flag is a true wetland plant: it wants consistently moist to
downright boggy soil and tolerates a few inches of standing water, making
it a natural for pond margins, rain gardens, low spots that never quite dry
out, or containers set in shallow water. Full sun brings out the strongest
variegation, though some afternoon shade is welcome in hot climates to keep
the leaf tips from scorching.

## Care tips

The single non-negotiable is moisture — this is not a plant to let dry out,
even briefly. Beyond that it is easygoing: average fertility is fine as long
as the site stays wet, it has no serious pests or diseases, and deer
generally leave it alone. Clean up any winter-damaged foliage in early
spring, and divide the clump every few years once it becomes congested.
