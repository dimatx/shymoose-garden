*Rhododendron* 'Karens' — commonly sold as the Karen azalea — is a compact, dense, broadleaf evergreen shrub and one of the most cold-hardy evergreen azaleas you can grow. Developed as part of Peter Girard's breeding program in Ohio, it was selected specifically for its ability to survive zone 4–5 winters while still delivering a spectacular spring flower display.

## A spring showstopper

In mid-spring, before the foliage has fully expanded, the entire shrub disappears beneath a smothering mass of fragrant, double (hose-in-hose) lavender-pink flowers. Each bloom is actually two flowers nested inside each other — a trait that gives them a fuller, ruffled look. The display lasts several weeks and draws in bees, butterflies, and hummingbirds. After the flowers fade, the small, glossy dark-green leaves take over for summer, then gradually deepen to a handsome reddish hue in fall and winter, giving the shrub year-round presence.

## Care tips

Karen azalea thrives in part shade with acidic, humus-rich, well-drained soil. Its shallow root system is the key to understanding its care: the roots dry out quickly and need consistent moisture, but they will rot if left sitting in standing water. A deep layer of mulch is the single most helpful thing you can do — it conserves moisture, keeps roots cool, moderates soil temperature, and slowly acidifies the soil as it breaks down.

Pruning is simple: trim lightly right after the spring flowers fade. Because next year's buds are set on the current year's growth through the summer, cutting in late summer or fall means cutting away next spring's show.

> **Note:** Karen azalea prefers a sheltered location — good air circulation helps prevent fungal issues, but protection from harsh winter winds reduces desiccation of the evergreen foliage.
