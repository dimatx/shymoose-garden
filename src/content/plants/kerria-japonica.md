*Kerria japonica* — Japanese rose or Easter rose — is a graceful deciduous shrub
from China and Japan, prized for its arching green stems and cheerful yellow
spring flowers. It is one of the few shrubs happy to bloom well in shade, making
it a reliable choice for the tricky spots other flowering shrubs skip.

## Spring gold in the shade

In mid-spring, slender arching canes are lined with bright yellow, rose-like
flowers about an inch or two across. Named cultivars offer double "pompon"
blooms or variegated foliage, but the straight species is prized for its
simplicity and its willingness to flower in partial to full shade where most
other flowering shrubs sulk.

## Care tips

Plant it in loamy, well-drained soil and give it shade from the hottest
afternoon sun, which otherwise bleaches the flowers. It is drought tolerant once
established and largely trouble-free, but it does sucker and spread over time —
plan to prune out old canes and unwanted suckers right after flowering, since
next year's blooms form on this year's wood. Even without leaves, the bright
green stems add welcome color to the winter garden.
