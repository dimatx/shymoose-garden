*Fuchsia magellanica* — Hardy Fuchsia, Magellan Fuchsia, or Hummingbird Fuchsia — is a deciduous to semi-evergreen shrub in the evening primrose family (Onagraceae), native to the cool, moist forests of southern South America, from Peru and Chile south to Tierra del Fuego. It is the hardiest species in the large *Fuchsia* genus, capable of surviving USDA zone 5 winters when mulched, making it the go-to choice for gardeners in northern climates who want the distinctive fuchsia flower without growing it as a tender annual.

## Lantern flowers from summer to frost

The genus honors Leonhart Fuchs (1501–1566), a German physician and herbalist, and the flowers are every bit as theatrical as their name suggests. Each bloom is a pendulous, two-part structure: reflexed scarlet-red sepals flair backward to reveal a nodding tube of rich purple-violet petals, below which hang long, elegant stamens and a pistil. They are produced continuously and prolifically from early summer through the first hard frost, transforming the arching stems into cascades of color. Hummingbirds home in on the nectar-rich flowers as reliably as any feeder; bees and other pollinators follow. Small, dark, juicy berries ripen after the flowers.

## Care tips

Hardy Fuchsia thrives in cool, moist conditions with dappled or partial shade — it tolerates full sun where summers are cool and humid, but afternoon shade is advisable in hot climates. Give it fertile, humus-rich soil and consistent moisture; it does not like drought, and it will wilt and decline in dry soil. Mulch generously to conserve moisture, cool the roots, and insulate them over winter.

In zones 5–6, the top growth is typically killed by frost but the roots survive under mulch and resprout vigorously in spring. In these climates, treat the dead stems as a late-spring cleanup task: wait until after the last frost date, confirm new growth is emerging from the base, and cut the old stems to the ground. In milder climates where stems survive the winter, prune hard in early spring to rejuvenate the plant and maximize the summer flower display.
