*Sisyrinchium angustifolium*, or blue-eyed grass, is a diminutive native
perennial that looks every bit like a fine-bladed grass — until it surprises
you with small, star-shaped blue-violet flowers with cheerful yellow centers.
It's a lovely, low-key addition to the front of a border, a rock garden, or
a naturalized meadow planting.

## A native masquerading as a grass

Despite the name and grass-like foliage, blue-eyed grass is actually a member
of the iris family. It naturalizes readily from seed, forming loose drifts
over time in meadows, along stream banks, and in moist open woodlands across
its native range in eastern and central North America.

## Care tips

Plant in full sun to partial shade in average, medium-moisture soil. It
tolerates occasional dry spells once established but performs best with
consistent moisture. Shear the clumps back lightly after the first flush of
flowers fades to tidy the foliage and often coax out a second round of bloom,
and let seed heads mature if you'd like it to spread naturally.
