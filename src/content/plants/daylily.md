*Hemerocallis lilioasphodelus* — commonly called the lemon daylily or yellow daylily — is a clump-forming herbaceous perennial in the family Asphodelaceae. Native across temperate Eurasia, it forms tidy mounds of arching, strap-shaped green leaves and is one of the earliest daylilies to flower each year.

## Fragrant early-summer blooms

In late spring and early summer, slender scapes rise 2–3 feet above the foliage carrying clear lemon-yellow, trumpet-shaped flowers roughly 3–4 inches across. Unlike most modern hybrids, this species is sweetly fragrant — the scent is most noticeable in the evening. Each bloom lasts only a single day, but each scape carries many buds, so the plant stays in flower for several weeks.

## Care tips

The lemon daylily is famously easygoing. It thrives in full sun to part shade and in almost any well-drained soil across a wide pH range. Young plants appreciate regular water while establishing, after which clumps tolerate drought well; the main thing to avoid is soggy winter soil, which can rot the crown. Deadhead spent scapes to keep things tidy, tidy up dead foliage in early spring, and divide congested clumps every few years to keep the display vigorous. It draws bees and butterflies, and its slow rhizomatous spread makes it a reliable choice for cottage borders and naturalistic plantings.
