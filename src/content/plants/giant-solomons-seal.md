*Polygonatum biflorum* var. *commutatum* — giant Solomon's seal — is a native
herbaceous perennial of moist eastern woodlands and the largest of the Solomon's
seals, reaching 3 to 5 feet and occasionally as much as 7. Tall, gracefully
arching stems carry broad, parallel-veined leaves with rows of greenish-white,
bell-shaped flowers dangling beneath them in late spring.

## Woodland presence

This is a plant for the back of a shady border, a woodland or rain garden, or a
spot where it can naturalize. Just give it plenty of room — it spreads slowly by
rhizomes and grows larger than other Solomon's seals. In fall the flowers give
way to showy blue-black berries (inedible to people, but loved by birds).

## Care tips

Plant it in average, moist to wet soil in partial to full shade; it does best
with a little sun. It has no serious pest or disease problems and asks for very
little once established.
