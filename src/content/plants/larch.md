*Larix decidua* 'Varied Directions' is an unusual, irregularly weeping selection of European larch — a species native to the mountainous Alps and Carpathians, but this particular cultivar has an all-American origin, bred by University of Connecticut horticulturist Dr. Sidney Waxman. Its branches sprawl and cascade unpredictably in every direction (hence the name), giving each specimen a one-of-a-kind sculptural form.

## A conifer that isn't evergreen

Larch belongs to a small group of deciduous conifers: its soft, bright green needles hold their color through summer, then turn a brilliant gold in autumn before dropping entirely for winter — a rare "fall color" show among cone-bearing trees. Small, hairy, upright cones and inconspicuous spring cone-flowers add subtle interest the rest of the year, but the tree's real appeal is its winding, ever-changing branch structure, especially striking against a bare winter sky.

## Care tips

'Varied Directions' needs full sun and moist, well-drained, gravelly or sandy soil — it performs poorly in heavy clay or hot, humid conditions. Because it's propagated by grafting, its ultimate shape depends heavily on how it's trained and staked while young: left alone it can sprawl into a low, wide mound, while staking encourages a taller, more tree-like specimen. Give it plenty of open space to show off its form, and prune only to remove dead or crossing wood once established.
