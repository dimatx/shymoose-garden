*Penstemon digitalis* 'Husker's Red' is a clump-forming herbaceous perennial in the plantain family (Plantaginaceae), a selection of the wild foxglove beardtongue native to eastern and central North America. Its signature feature is colour: the new foliage emerges deep maroon in spring, ages to green veined with maroon, and the wiry flower stems stay a glossy burgundy all season — a striking foil for the flowers.

## Early-summer bloom

In late spring to early summer, branching panicles open dozens of white, two-lipped tubular flowers, often blushed pink with fine purple nectar lines inside the lower lip. The tube is perfectly sized for bees and hummingbirds. After the petals drop, the rust-coloured seed capsules carry interest well into winter.

## Care tips

This is an easy, drought-tolerant native once established. Give it full sun and well-drained soil — the foliage colours best in sun, and good drainage prevents the winter root rot that is its only real weakness. Leave the seed heads standing through winter, cut back in late winter, and divide every few years. Deer leave it alone, and it earns its keep in pollinator beds, cottage borders, rock gardens and naturalized plantings.
