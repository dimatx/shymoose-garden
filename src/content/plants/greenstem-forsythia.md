*Forsythia viridissima* — Greenstem Forsythia, also called Golden Bells — is a deciduous shrub in the olive family (Oleaceae), native to south China and South Korea. The species name *viridissima* means "very green," referring to the distinctively green stems that set it apart from the more commonly planted hybrid forsythias.

## The original forsythia

While hybrid forsythias have largely taken over the trade, Greenstem Forsythia is the straight species from which many cultivars descend. It grows into a dense, arching mound of multi-stemmed branches up to 6–10 feet tall, with long lance-shaped leaves that develop a hint of purple in fall. In late February through April — before a single leaf unfurls — the entire shrub erupts in bright yellow 4-lobed flowers, creating a vivid splash of color when little else in the garden stirs.

## Tough and adaptable

Few flowering shrubs are as undemanding. Greenstem Forsythia tolerates clay, poor soil, urban compaction, drought, salt spray, and deer browsing. The arching stems are excellent for cutting and can be brought indoors in late winter to force blooms weeks early in a warm vase. 

The one critical care rule: prune only immediately after flowering. Forsythia blooms on the previous year's wood, so pruning in summer or fall cuts away the very buds that will flower next spring. A hard renovation prune right after bloom — cutting older stems to the ground — keeps the shrub dense and maximises the annual flower show.
