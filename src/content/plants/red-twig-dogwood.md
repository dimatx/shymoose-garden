Red-twig dogwood (also called red osier dogwood) is a native, multi-stemmed,
deciduous shrub found across most of North America, typically along
streambanks, wetland margins, and other damp, low-lying ground. This compact
selection keeps all of the species' best traits — the brilliant red winter
stems, wildlife value, and toughness — in a smaller, tidier package suited to
an ordinary garden bed rather than a wild thicket.

## Through the seasons

Small, fragrant white flowers appear in flattened clusters in late spring,
followed by white berries (sometimes tinged blue) that songbirds relish
through summer and fall. The medium-green leaves turn shades of orange, red,
or purple in autumn. Once the leaves drop, the shrub's true feature takes
over: bare stems that glow bright red all winter, especially striking against
snow.

## Care tips

This is an easy, adaptable shrub as long as it gets consistent moisture —
it's an excellent choice for a rain garden, low spot, or streamside planting,
and it tolerates clay and even occasional standing water without complaint.
Give it full sun for the most vivid stem color. The single most important
task is renewal pruning: cut about a fifth to a quarter of the oldest stems
to the ground each spring, since the brightest red color comes from young
growth and fades as stems age.

> **Heads up:** This species can be stressed by hot, humid summers south of
> its comfort zone (roughly USDA zone 7). In warmer climates, give it some
> afternoon shade and consistent moisture to keep it healthy.
