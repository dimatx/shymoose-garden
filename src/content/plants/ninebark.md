Ninebark is a tough, adaptable, native deciduous shrub found along stream
banks and rocky hillsides across central and eastern North America. This
dwarf cultivar packs the species' best traits — dark, colorful foliage,
dainty spring flowers, and shaggy exfoliating bark — into a compact form
suited to smaller gardens.

## Through the seasons

Small, five-petaled flowers open from darker pink buds into rounded, spirea-
like clusters in late spring, standing out against foliage that runs from
deep maroon to bronze all season long. The color holds up especially well in
full sun. As flowers fade, drooping clusters of reddish seed capsules take
over, feeding birds into fall. Once leaves drop, older stems reveal
attractive bark that peels away in thin, reddish-brown strips — the source
of the plant's common name.

## Care tips

Ninebark is famously low-fuss: it tolerates dry spells, clay soil, and urban
conditions with little complaint, and disease resistance (including to
powdery mildew) is one of this cultivar's selling points. Because flowers
form on old wood, prune right after blooming rather than in late winter or
spring, which would remove the coming season's buds. An overgrown or tired
plant can be cut back hard in late winter to rejuvenate it from the base.

> **Heads up:** Purple- and maroon-leaved ninebark cultivars like this one
> hold their darkest color with more direct sun — in heavy shade, the foliage
> fades toward green.
