*Hydrangea arborescens* is a multi-stemmed deciduous shrub native to moist woods, rocky slopes, and ravine banks throughout the eastern United States — making it one of the most cold-hardy hydrangeas you can grow. From early summer through fall, it erupts in massive rounded clusters of creamy-white flowers that start out pale green, age through white, and eventually dry to a warm tan. Unlike the more commonly planted bigleaf hydrangeas (*H. macrophylla*), Smooth Hydrangea flowers on new wood each year, so late-winter pruning — even cutting it close to the ground — is rewarded with a full flush of blooms in the same season.

## A reliable native for shade gardens

Smooth Hydrangea is one of the best-performing native shrubs for partially shaded spots in eastern North American gardens. It tolerates clay soils, rain gardens, and sloped sites, and its peeling, multi-layered bark (which gave rise to the folk name "sevenbark") adds textural interest in winter. Named cultivars like 'Annabelle' and 'Incrediball' produce flower heads up to 12 inches across, though the stems may flop after heavy rain on unpruned plants; cutting them back hard each spring solves the problem.

## Care tips

Plant in partial shade in average to moist, well-drained soil. Water regularly until established. Prune in late winter before buds break — the harder you cut, the bigger the flowers, though fewer stems may mean more flopping. Divide clumps every few years to keep them contained if spreading is a concern.
