*Phalaris arundinacea* 'Picta' — ribbon grass or gardener's garters — is a rhizomatous perennial grass (Poaceae) grown for its boldly variegated foliage: upright blades striped lengthwise in white and green that brighten a border, a damp margin or a difficult dry corner. It is one of the toughest, most adaptable ornamentals you can plant.

## Foliage, not flowers

The summer flower panicles are slim and forgettable; the point of this grass is the crisp white-and-green striping, which reads almost silver from a distance and lights up shade. In cool spring weather the new growth often carries a pink flush before settling to white and green.

## Care tips — and a warning

Ribbon grass will grow almost anywhere, which is both its strength and its catch: it spreads vigorously by underground rhizomes and the plain green wild form is an aggressive wetland invader. Plant 'Picta' where you can contain it — a sunken pot, a bed bounded by paving, or a spot you actively want covered — and avoid letting it escape near natural waterways. Cut it to the ground in late winter, and shear it back midseason if the leaves brown to bring on fresh foliage. Beyond keeping it in bounds, it asks for nothing.
