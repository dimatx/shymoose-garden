The American royal fern (*Osmunda spectabilis*, formerly *Osmunda regalis*
var. *spectabilis*) is a deciduous fern native to eastern North America. Its broad,
finely divided fronds make an elegant clump in wet woodland, streamside, and
shaded garden settings.

## A fern for moist shade

Royal fern grows best in rich, acidic soil that stays consistently moist or
wet. It usually forms a 2–5-foot clump, and can grow taller where moisture is
abundant. The green fronds turn yellow to golden brown in fall; fertile fronds
carry distinctive brown, tassel-like spore clusters at their tips.

## Care tips

Site it in part to full shade, especially where summers are hot. In a cool
climate it can take more sun when its roots remain moist. Cut away old fronds
in early spring before the new growth unfurls.

For pet safety, [Gardeners' World](https://www.gardenersworld.com/plants/osmunda-regalis/)
reports no toxic effects for *Osmunda regalis*, including cats and dogs.
Our plant is *O. spectabilis*, formerly classified as a variety of that
species; this related-plant assessment is not a species-specific veterinary
clearance. Do not feed it to pets or assume that all ferns are safe to eat.

Planting distance follows the mature width in
[NC State Extension's American royal fern profile](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/osmunda-spectabilis/).
Division and spore-sowing advice follows the
[RHS royal fern profile](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/12001/osmunda-regalis/details)
for the closely related *Osmunda regalis*; the American species was formerly
classified as *O. regalis* var. *spectabilis*.
