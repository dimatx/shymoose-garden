*Astilbe* 'Rock and Roll' is a hybrid false spirea (Saxifragaceae), one of the *Astilbe* × *arendsii* group developed from Asian species by the German nurseryman Georg Arends. It forms a tidy clump of glossy, deeply cut, fern-like foliage on reddish stems and sends up bold, fluffy plumes of clear-white flowers — a bright, cooling presence in a shady border.

## Plumes for the shade

In early to midsummer, tall pyramidal panicles packed with hundreds of tiny white flowers open from the bottom up, giving a long-lasting display that's excellent for cutting. As the flowers fade they dry to a soft bronze and hold their shape, carrying interest well into winter. The ferny foliage stays handsome all season as long as the plant has enough moisture.

## Care tips

Moisture is everything with astilbe: it thrives in the damp, humus-rich soil of a woodland edge, a rain garden, or a pondside, and it sulks — browning at the leaf margins — wherever the ground dries out. Give it partial or dappled shade (or full sun only if you can keep it constantly moist), feed the soil with compost, and divide the clump every few years in spring. Deer and rabbits leave it alone, and butterflies visit the plumes.
