*Acer palmatum* 'Crimson Queen' is a weeping laceleaf Japanese maple prized above almost all other ornamental trees for its extraordinary grace. Native to Japan, China, and Korea, it has been cultivated in Japanese gardens for centuries and remains one of the most sought-after small trees in the world.

## Stunning foliage all season long

'Crimson Queen' holds its deeply dissected, seven-lobed crimson-red leaves from spring bud-break straight through summer — a feat that distinguishes it from many other Japanese maples whose red foliage fades to green in the heat. In autumn the color intensifies to brilliant scarlet before the leaves fall, revealing the elegant arching branch structure beneath. Tiny red-purple flowers appear in mid-spring, followed by ornamental winged samaras (seed pods) that ripen to reddish brown.

## A specimen for any garden

Its slow growth and compact weeping mound (typically 4–6 feet tall and 6–8 feet wide at maturity) make 'Crimson Queen' ideal for small gardens, containers, courtyards, and as a focal point in woodland or Asian-inspired plantings. It layers beautifully as an understory tree beneath taller canopy trees, which also provide the dappled shade it prefers. The plant is mildly resistant to deer browsing.

## Care tips

Plant in a sheltered spot with dappled sunlight and protection from hot afternoon sun and drying winds — both can scorch the delicate foliage. Provide consistently moist, well-drained soil rich in organic matter, and mulch generously to keep the shallow roots cool and moist. Pruning needs are minimal; remove dead or crossing wood in late winter while the tree is dormant. Avoid heavy pruning, which can stress the plant and disfigure its natural form.
