*Rodgersia aesculifolia* is one of those dramatic foliage perennials that can
make a temperate shade garden feel almost subtropical. Each leaf is divided
into broad, pleated leaflets like a horse chestnut, and the whole clump reads
as bold and architectural even before it flowers.

In early to midsummer, airy branching plumes of tiny creamy-white to soft
pinkish flowers rise above the foliage. The bloom is attractive, but the real
show is the combination of those flower clouds with the oversized, heavily
textured leaves, especially in a woodland border or beside water.

Give fingerleaf rodgersia what it wants most — rich soil that never dries out.
It excels in part shade, rain-garden edges, and pondside plantings, where the
root zone stays cool and damp. In dry ground or hot sun the leaf edges scorch,
so this is a plant to site generously and water faithfully rather than test for
toughness.
