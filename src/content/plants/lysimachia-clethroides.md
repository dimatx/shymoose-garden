*Lysimachia clethroides* — gooseneck loosestrife — is a herbaceous perennial from
East Asia grown for its distinctive summer flower spikes: slender white racemes
that arch over at the tip so every bloom faces the same direction, exactly like a
goose craning its neck. It is easy, showy, and utterly undemanding.

## An easy summer show

From midsummer into early fall the plant sends up dozens of curved white spikes
that sway above the foliage and draw in bees and butterflies. It asks for little
beyond average garden care and shrugs off heat, humidity, and short dry spells
once established.

## Care tips — and a word of caution

Give it full sun and moist, humusy soil for the showiest bloom; it is equally at
home at a pond's edge or in an ordinary border. The one thing to plan for is its
vigorous spread by underground rhizomes — gooseneck loosestrife can travel well
beyond where it was planted and crowd out neighbors if left alone. Site it where
it can roam, contain it with a buried barrier, or divide it regularly to keep it
in check.
