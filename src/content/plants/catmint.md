*Nepeta* × *faassenii* 'Junior Walker' is a compact cultivar of Faasen's catmint, a sterile hybrid perennial in the mint family (Lamiaceae). A garden cross of *Nepeta racemosa* and *N. nepetella* first raised at Faassen Nurseries in the Netherlands, 'Junior Walker' is valued for its tidier, lower-growing habit compared to the taller 'Walker's Low' — making it ideal for the front of a border, edging paths and walkways, and containers. As a sterile hybrid that sets no seed, it channels all its energy into flowering rather than fruiting, which means it blooms for a very long time.

## Fragrant foliage, long-season color

The plant forms a low, spreading mound of softly aromatic grey-green leaves that billow pleasantly over the edge of a path or bed. From late spring through midsummer, the stems are blanketed in small, two-lipped lavender-blue flowers that bees visit constantly. Cats are inevitably drawn to the crushed foliage — the scent, which to humans is pleasantly minty, is irresistible to felines, who will roll in it and chew on stems given the chance. Deer, however, dislike the fragrance and give it a wide berth.

## Care tips

Catmint is one of the most undemanding garden perennials. It thrives in full sun with good drainage and is highly drought tolerant once established. In the South, light afternoon shade helps in the hottest months.

The one key care task is the midsummer shearing: once the first flush of bloom fades, cut the whole plant back by one-third to one-half. Within a few weeks, it rebushes with fresh foliage and a full second wave of flowers that often carries through to frost. Skip the shearing and the plant looks tatty for the rest of the season. The only failure mode is wet, poorly drained winter soil, which can cause crown rot — ensure good drainage and the plant is virtually maintenance-free.
