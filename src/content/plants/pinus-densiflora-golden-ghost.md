*Pinus densiflora* 'Golden Ghost' is a variegated form of the Japanese red pine,
grown for the striking effect of its soft, two-needle bundles brightly banded in
yellow and creamy white. From a distance the whole plant seems to glow — a
luminous, almost ghostly presence among darker evergreens — which is exactly the
role it plays best in the garden.

## A bright, well-behaved evergreen

Unlike the towering 40–60 foot species, 'Golden Ghost' is a slow, restrained
grower, reaching only about 6 feet tall in its first decade in a broadly upright,
oval form. That measured pace and modest size make it a manageable specimen or
accent for a mixed border, a conifer bed, or a Japanese-style garden, where its
variegation reads especially well against blue spruces and dark green foliage.

## Giving it what it needs

Plant it in full sun for the most vivid color and densest growth, in soil that
drains freely — it is unfussy about fertility and tolerates poor, sandy ground
once established. Little pruning is required; if you want to slow or shape it,
pinch the soft spring candles rather than cutting into old wood.
