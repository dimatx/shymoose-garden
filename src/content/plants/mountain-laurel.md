Mountain laurel is a broadleaf evergreen shrub native to eastern North
America, where it grows on rocky slopes, acidic woodland margins, and
mountain balds. This cultivar keeps the species' hallmark good looks —
leathery, dark green leaves year-round and clusters of elaborately patterned
flowers each late spring — in a more compact, garden-friendly size.

## Through the seasons

In late spring to early summer, terminal clusters of cup-shaped flowers open
from pink buds, each bloom marked on the inside with delicate cinnamon-purple
banding. The flowers have a clever mechanical trick: ten anthers are held
under tension in small pockets around the corolla, and when a visiting bee
brushes one, the filament springs free and flings pollen onto the bee. The
glossy, elliptic evergreen foliage stays handsome through the rest of the
year, and older plants develop gnarled, twisting trunks that add winter
character.

## Care tips

Give mountain laurel cool, consistently moist, acidic soil and a spot with
morning sun and afternoon shade — the classic woodland-edge conditions it
evolved in. It resents heavy clay or waterlogged ground, so improve drainage
or plant on a raised bed if needed, and mulch to keep the root zone cool.
Prune right after flowering if you want to shape it or remove spent blooms;
since next year's flower buds form on this year's growth, a late pruning
sacrifices the coming display.

> **Heads up:** Every part of this plant is toxic if ingested, and it carries
> a high flammability rating — keep it out of the defensible space immediately
> around your home, and keep pets and children from chewing on it.
