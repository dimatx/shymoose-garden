*Hydrangea quercifolia* 'Ruby Slippers' is a compact selection of the native
oakleaf hydrangea, a deciduous shrub of the southeastern United States. It
keeps the species' distinctive lobed leaves and peeling bark in a smaller
plant suited to gardens where a full-size oakleaf hydrangea would take up too
much room.

## Flowers and fall color

In early summer, upright, cone-shaped panicles open white, then shift through
pink to ruby red as they mature. The deeply lobed foliage turns mahogany red
in autumn, and mature stems reveal attractive exfoliating bark in winter.

## Care tips

Plant in organically rich, well-drained soil and keep it evenly moist,
especially while it establishes. Full sun to part shade suits the shrub, with
afternoon shade helpful in hot summers. It flowers on old wood, so prune only
just after flowering if shaping is necessary; pruning later can remove next
year's buds.

The [U.S. National Arboretum introduction sheet](https://usna.usda.gov/assets/images/as_standard_image/Hydrangea_quercifolia_Ruby_Slippers.pdf)
describes propagation by softwood cuttings, rooting in 4–6 weeks under mist.
