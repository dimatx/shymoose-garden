*Campanula glomerata* 'Superba' — Clustered Bellflower, also known as Dane's Blood — is an upright, clumping herbaceous perennial in the bellflower family (Campanulaceae), native to meadows, open woodlands, and rocky slopes across most of Europe and temperate western Asia. 'Superba' is the tallest and most floriferous of the named cultivars, sending up stems to 2–3 feet in flower with large, intensely colored violet-purple clusters.

## Strikingly clustered blooms

The common and scientific names describe the plant's most distinctive feature: the flowers are tightly bunched into spherical heads (the epithet *glomerata* means "gathered into a ball") atop medium-green, lance-shaped, slightly hairy stems. Each terminal cluster can hold up to 15 upward-facing, bell-shaped flowers, creating a bold, almost geometric effect. Smaller clusters bloom simultaneously from the upper leaf axils, extending the display from May into July. The vivid blooms attract bees, butterflies, hummingbirds, and specialized plasterer bees, while deer and rabbits tend to leave the plant alone.

## Care tips

Plant in average, consistently moist, well-drained soil in full sun or partial shade. In hot, humid climates, afternoon shade extends bloom and prevents stress — the plant does not tolerate drought. Deadheading spent flower stems promptly and reliably triggers a second flush later in summer.

The main caution with Clustered Bellflower is its enthusiasm for spreading: it spreads by rhizomes and can naturalize aggressively, particularly in moist soils, and may also self-seed. Dividing the clumps every 3–5 years in fall keeps growth contained, prevents die-out at the center, and produces plenty of new plants for elsewhere in the garden.
