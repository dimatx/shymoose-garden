*Picea sitchensis* 'Silberzwerg' is a dwarf form of Sitka spruce — a species that,
in the wild, ranks among the tallest conifers on the planet — bred down into a
dense, slow-growing mound for small gardens and containers. Its claim to fame is
its foliage: each needle is a striking two-tone, silvery-blue on the upper
surface and dark green beneath, giving the whole plant a shimmering, frosted look
that shifts with the light and the angle you view it from.

## A tiny fraction of a giant

In the wild, Sitka spruce grows along a narrow strip of the Pacific coast from
Alaska to northern California, rarely straying far from the ocean's moderating
influence, and can exceed 200 feet. 'Silberzwerg' inherits none of that scale —
it creeps along at just 3 to 6 inches a year, taking on a broad, upright habit
and reaching only about 8 feet tall and 6 feet wide after decades in the
garden, making it a genuine dwarf conifer rather than simply a "slow" one.

## Care tips

Plant in full sun in moist, well-drained, slightly acidic soil for the densest
growth and best color; it will tolerate some afternoon shade but grows looser
there. Water regularly while it establishes, keep it out of soggy, poorly
drained spots, and otherwise leave it be — pruning is limited to removing any
dead or damaged wood. Watch for spruce aphids, adelgids, and spider mites, which
can occasionally trouble this species.
