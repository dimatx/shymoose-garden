Hollywood juniper is an upright evergreen conifer in the cypress family,
grown not for flowers but for its irregular, sculptural form: slightly
twisted branches carry dense, puffy clusters of scale-like foliage that
read almost like a living topiary. Introduced to the U.S. from Japan in the
1920s, it became a signature plant of mid-century Southern California
landscaping — hence the common name.

## Through the seasons

The bright to dark green, scale-like foliage stays attractive year-round,
with no real seasonal change beyond the slow thickening of its sculptural
branches. As a dioecious conifer, female plants may bear small, whitish-blue,
berry-like seed cones that deepen to a violet-brown over about two years,
though the plant's real appeal is its form rather than any bloom or fruit
display.

## Care tips

Give this juniper full sun and well-drained soil and it will largely take
care of itself — it tolerates heat, drought, salt spray, alkaline soil, and
poor urban conditions once established, and rarely needs pruning beyond the
occasional removal of dead wood. Because its irregular, twisting silhouette
is the whole point, avoid shearing it into a formal shape; that flattens the
very character that makes it a "Hollywood" juniper. Its irregular growth
also makes it a poor choice for a tight hedge — plant it instead as a
specimen, an accent near a gate or entrance, or a focal point in an Asian- or
Mediterranean-style garden.

> **Heads up:** The one thing this plant won't tolerate is wet feet. Avoid
> low spots where water collects, and skip the daily watering habit
> favored by more moisture-loving plants nearby.
